# Glossary

Plain-English meanings of the terms you'll see.

**52-week range.** The lowest and highest price over the past year.

**Analyst rating.** Professional analysts' average opinion, from 1 (strong buy) to 5 (sell).

**Benchmark.** What a stock is compared against, usually the whole market, such as the S&P 500 for the US or the Nifty 50 for India.

**Bull case / bear case.** The best argument for the stock going up (bull) and for it going down (bear).

**Current ratio.** Short-term assets divided by short-term debts. Above 1.5 means the company can comfortably pay its bills.

**Debt-to-equity (D/E).** How much the company owes compared with what it owns outright. Under 0.5 is low, and over 2 is heavy.

**Earnings.** A company's profit. Companies report earnings every quarter, and the stock often jumps or drops sharply on that day.

**Earnings surprise.** How much actual profit beat (or missed) what analysts expected.

**EPS (earnings per share).** Profit divided by the number of shares.

**Free cash flow (FCF).** Cash left over after running and investing in the business. Positive is good.

**Market cap.** The total value of all a company's shares (price × number of shares).

**Momentum.** Whether the price has been trending up or down recently.

**Moving average (50-day, 200-day).** The average price over the last 50 or 200 trading days. A price above its 200-day average usually means an uptrend.

**P/E ratio (price-to-earnings).** Share price divided by yearly profit per share. It tells you how many years of current profit you're paying for. Lower is cheaper; a negative P/E means the company is losing money.

**Forward P/E.** P/E using *expected* profit for next year instead of last year's.

**PEG ratio.** P/E divided by the growth rate. Around 1 is often considered fair for a growing company.

**Price target.** Where analysts think the price will be in about 12 months.

**Profit margin.** How much of each rupee or dollar of sales is kept as profit. 25% means 25 cents of every dollar.

**ROE (return on equity).** How much profit the company makes from shareholders' money. Above 15% is good.

**RSI (Relative Strength Index).** A 0–100 measure of how fast the price has been moving. Over 70 is "overbought" (may have risen too fast); under 30 is "oversold".

**Support / resistance.** Price levels where a stock has tended to stop falling (support) or stop rising (resistance).

**Ticker.** A stock's short code, like `AAPL` or `RELIANCE.NS`. See [Tickers and markets](./guide/tickers).
